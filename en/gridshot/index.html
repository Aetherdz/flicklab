What Gridshot trains

With three targets live at once, the click you are making is never the one you are aiming. You scan, choose, flick, confirm, and start again immediately. That loop trains target switching under time pressure — the part of aim that decides a duel before the first shot — more than it trains a single rehearsed flick.

The grid is deliberately plain. No scenery, no weapon sway, no movement mechanics to learn. What is left is the thing you actually want to measure: how fast your hand finds a target it did not choose a moment ago.

How scoring works

A fast but sloppy run and a slower accurate run can land in the same place, which is the point: the score is a single number standing in for two habits that pull against each other. Read accuracy before you read score.

Score
Hits multiplied by the difficulty value (Easy 1, Normal 1.5, Hard 2), times ten.
Accuracy
Every click counts, including clicks that land on an empty cell.
Time to target
Measured from the moment a target appears to the timestamp of the click that removes it, taken from the browser input event rather than a render frame.
Streak
Consecutive hits. One click on an empty cell resets it.

Three habits that make you faster

  • Move your eyes first. Looking at the next target before your hand leaves the current one removes the pause you cannot see in your own aim.
  • Aim at the near edge of a target, not its centre. It is a shorter distance that ends in the same hit.
  • Click once. A second click on a target that has already respawned is a miss, and misses cost more than they look like they do.

How to practise

Three 60-second runs with a short rest between them is a session. Longer sessions train stamina, not precision, and the numbers stop improving once your forearm tightens. Come back tomorrow: the gains in this exercise show up across days, not inside one sitting.

Change one variable at a time. Run the same difficulty and duration for a week, then move one step — smaller targets or a longer run — and watch which of your numbers moves first.

Questions

Is this the same as Aim Lab or KovaaK's?

No. Those are 3D trainers with weapon handling and imported scenarios. FlickLab is a browser trainer for short daily reps, with plain 2D geometry so the only thing being measured is your aim.

What is a good Gridshot score?

Compare against your own average for the same difficulty and duration, not against a stranger's. The score is built from hits and misses, so two very different runs can produce the same number.

Does a gaming mouse matter?

A wired mouse with a high polling rate removes one source of noise from the measurement. It will not fix accuracy; it only stops the hardware from hiding it.
