What this test measures

Clicks per second is a hardware-and-hand metric, not an aim metric. It is here because click speed is a real input limit for some games and because it is useful to know your own number when a weapon's fire rate is faster than your finger.

The pad is a fixed square in the middle of the field. Clicks inside it count; clicks outside count as misses and lower accuracy. That keeps the test about a stable hand rather than about dragging the cursor around the screen.

How scoring works

Score
Clicks per second multiplied by 100 and by the difficulty value (Easy 1, Normal 1.5, Hard 2).
Duration
1, 5, 10, 30 or 60 seconds. Short runs measure a burst; long runs measure a rate you can hold.
Debounce
Two clicks closer than 20 milliseconds apart are treated as one, which filters hardware double-click bounce without capping a real rate.
Accuracy
Clicks that land outside the pad count as misses.

Burst versus sustained

A one-second run is a burst: most people can produce a much higher number for one second than for thirty. Neither number is wrong, but they answer different questions. If you care about a game where you hold fire, the 30 second number is the honest one.

The score weight is the same for every duration, so a 60-second run is not automatically worth more points. Compare like with like: the results chart groups runs by mode and difficulty, but not by duration, so keep the duration fixed when you are tracking progress.

How to practise

  • Use a relaxed finger and let the switch reset. Tensing the forearm produces a fast first second and a slow rest of the run.
  • Keep the cursor still. Moving between clicks adds distance to every click and costs rate.
  • If you use a mechanical switch, note its actuation point; a shorter travel usually means a higher sustained rate.

Questions

Does CPS matter for aim?

Only for games where fire rate is limited by clicking. For tracking and flicking, the movement is the bottleneck, not the click.

Why are two very fast clicks merged?

Mechanical switches bounce, and a single physical click can produce two electrical events. The 20 ms floor removes that noise while still allowing up to 50 clicks per second.

Why does the pad have a difficulty setting?

It changes the pad size. A smaller pad requires a steadier hand at the same click rate, which makes the score multiplier meaningful.
